# Theme 1 – Job-search sources and trends, CV, personal brand, cover letter

## 1. Job-search sources
- **Sources** = channels through which candidates reach job offers. They don't show everything: ~**80 % of offers belong to the hidden job market** (*mercado oculto de empleo*): filled through contacts, internal recommendations or direct selection of known candidates. → Networking and self-application are indispensable.

### 1.1–1.2 Job portals (*portales de empleo*)
- Types: **meta-search** (scrape other portals, e.g. Trovit, Jobrapido), **general** (all sectors), **specialised** (by sector or group: tech, tourism, first job, executives…). Set up **email alerts**; most have mobile apps.
- General: InfoJobs (most known), Infoempleo, Monster, Trabajos.com, Jobandtalent (algorithm-personalised offers). Specialised: Studentjob, Primerempleo, Turijobs, Luxetalent, AnyWorkAnywhere (temporary jobs abroad).
- Tip: don't mass-apply; duplicate applications and offers you don't really want hurt you.

### 1.3 Career-guidance sites
- **SEPE** (state public employment service: guidance + training for unemployed), **Cámara de Comercio** (local offers + training).

### 1.4 Networking
- Strategy of using personal/professional contacts to reach non-public opportunities. (Rosaura Alastruey: 75–80 % of offers are filled via contacts.)
- Must be organised: calendar of events (fairs, conferences, courses, webinars), choose the right social networks, keep profiles active (abandoned profiles = disinterest).
- Networks: **LinkedIn** (main professional network), Facebook (job groups), Instagram/X (follow industry leaders), Glassdoor (company reviews, interview prep), Indeed.
- **Digital footprint** (*huella digital*): trail left by what we post/share. Can't be erased, but can be reviewed and shaped; recruiters look beyond compromising photos (who we follow, what we share). Separate personal vs. professional footprint; check privacy settings.

### 1.5 Labour trends (World Economic Forum)
- Generative **AI** reshapes work unevenly; digital jobs keep growing; unemployment may rise unless **reskilling / lifelong learning**; more temporary, location-bound contracts → mobility; **soft skills** gaining weight (leadership, teamwork, resilience, critical thinking); work–life balance measures.

## 2. Job offers: selection, analysis, self-application
- Some offers don't fit, have poor conditions, or are scams (pyramid schemes) → select carefully.
- **Analysis checklist:** (1) research the company (mission, values, culture, innovation, development, position in sector); (2) match your skills/experience to the requirements; (3) your professional goals (short/long term, environment, tasks, tech + soft skills); (4) contract conditions (contract type, salary, schedule/flexibility, location/remote…).
- SEPE *Observatorio de las Ocupaciones* studies professional profiles from published offers.
- **Case (Javier / TechSolutions):** admin-assistant offer evaluated against his profile and interests.
- **Self-application** (*autocandidatura*): applying directly to a company with no published offer → access to hidden market; LinkedIn can act as a permanent self-application.
  - Steps: research the company, find the right person/department, tailor the application; list target companies in a table; know their **company culture** (shared values, beliefs, behaviours, norms).

## 3. The CV (*currículum vitae*)
- Purpose: present skills concisely so you're called to interview, not discarded. Personal, but must fit the target job.
- **Types:** chronological (by date; solid continuous record / recent graduates), functional (by skill; gaps or sector change), combined, creative (design/marketing; don't sacrifice clarity), **European/Europass** (standardised, for working abroad), **video CV** (complements, never replaces).
- **Structure (minimum):** personal data/contact + links (LinkedIn) · personal description/profile · work experience (reverse chronological: company, role, tasks/achievements) · education (institution, title, dates) · skills (technical + soft) · languages (level) · courses/extra training · optional: driving licence, etc.
- Templates: Canva, Google Docs, Word, Zety, Europass.
- **Presentation:** clean, professional layout; legible font; no excess colours; **no spelling errors**; short (focus on what's relevant to the offer); save as **PDF**; proofread / have someone review.
- **Tips:** keep CV and online profiles updated; add a cover letter with keywords from the offer; photo is optional and depends on sector/country (must be professional).

## 4. Personal brand (*marca personal*)
- Set of attributes, skills and values that define you professionally – your "shop window". Must serve the goal: finding employment.
- Social media works both ways (companies look for staff, candidates for offers) – but what shows about you may not fit the image you want → define the brand deliberately (values, what makes you unique, how you want to be seen).
- **SWOT/DAFO** applied to job search: Strengths and Weaknesses (internal), Opportunities and Threats (external). Recruiters also research candidates online.
- **Objectives:** build your brand (control what others think of you) and **networking** (grow contacts).
- **Strategies:** pick fitting platforms (LinkedIn for professional), publish relevant content (articles, infographics, sector news), comment/share others' posts, join groups and debates, keep a regular, coherent presence; protect personal data, adjust privacy, get consent before sharing third-party info.
- Practice: build a SWOT matrix for a given case (Clara).

## 5. Cover letter (*carta de presentación*)
- Accompanies the CV; first impression. **≤ 1 page**, don't reuse the same letter for different applications, adapt to the company, simple language, no spelling mistakes.
- **Structure:** header (name, contact, date, recipient) → formal greeting ("Estimado/a…") → intro (who you are, which position, where you found it) → body (why this job/company; most relevant skills/experience – internships, school projects) → closing (thanks, wish for an interview) → formal sign-off ("Atentamente").
- An email can replace the letter when sending the CV: concise, professional, personalised.
- **Practical case:** correct a weak letter (vague "I like technology", no concrete skills, no specific interest in the company) → be specific about what attracts you to the company and give concrete evidence of skills.

# Theme 2 – In-demand profiles, selection processes, interviews, Europe

## 1. Most in-demand profiles
- **Job deposits** (*yacimientos de empleo*): sectors/activities with high potential to create new jobs because of social, technological and economic change (digitalisation, sustainability, AI, green economy, Industry 4.0, remote work, ageing population).
- Tech sector keeps growing in Spain (AI / machine-learning specialists).
- Demanded profiles by area (FP titles): **tech** (DAM, ASIR, SMR, 3D animation/games), **health & social care**, **sustainability / renewables / environment**, **logistics & supply chain**, **finance & risk / insurance / admin management**.

## 2. Recruitment techniques (*reclutamiento*)
- Recruitment = process to attract candidates for vacancies.
- **Internal** (promotions/transfers: motivation, loyalty, lower costs) vs. **external**:
  - Job portals (LinkedIn, InfoJobs), **job fairs** (meet in person, practical skills), **social networks** (attract *passive talent*), **employee referrals**, **headhunters / recruitment agencies** (specialised/senior profiles), **temporary-contract recruitment** (covering leave, etc.), tech tools (AI to filter CVs and assess competences; AI can reduce human bias).

### 2.1 Selection processes
- **Private sector** (flexible; in-person or online): job interviews; **psychometric & skills tests** (personality, numerical aptitude, IT skills, language tests); **group dynamics** (observe interaction, communication, leadership).
- **Public sector** (regulated: transparency, equality, merit): **concurso público** (merit scoring: experience, training, languages – no exam) and **oposición** (exams testing knowledge).
- Inclusive hiring policies fight discrimination.
- **New trends:** AI & selection algorithms (screen CVs, predict performance), **online interviews** (Zoom/Teams, boosted by COVID), **digital-skills tests**, and **personal-brand / social-media analysis** of candidates (digital coherence).
- Example (TalentoTec): AI CV filtering → practical coding test → Teams interview with HR → LinkedIn profile review.

### 2.2 Strategies to pass selection (Fig. 2.5)
- (A) Know the process in depth (tests, requirements, syllabus, deadlines, evaluation criteria).
- (B) Academic/technical preparation (study plan, software, languages, extra courses/internships).
- Plus: communication, personal-brand care, networking, emotional management (relaxation techniques, learning from failure), awareness of sector trends.

### 2.3 The job interview
- Formal conversation between a candidate and a company representative. For first jobs/internships, interviewers value **attitude, willingness to learn, adaptability** over experience.
- **Types:** by participants – *individual* / *group*; by format – *in person* / *online*; by questions – *behavioural*, *competency-based*, *situational (case)*.
- Prepare, mind **non-verbal communication** (eye contact, posture).

### The STAR method
- Structure answers about past experience: **S**ituation → **T**ask → **A**ction (what *you* did) → **R**esult (with data/achievements).
- Case (Mateo, ASIR graduate, IT-support interview): describes a group network project (intermittent connection failures) – routing/configuration work, spotting inconsistencies, documenting – result: delivered a robust solution and gained troubleshooting + cybersecurity skills.

### Typical questions (Fig. 2.7)
- **Technical/profile:** why this specialisation, what you learned in internships, strengths/weaknesses, tools used, biggest technical challenge, keeping up to date, safety/quality procedures.
- **Attitude/interpersonal:** how you work in a team, conflict handling, organisation/time, reaction to criticism, responsibility for mistakes.
- **Availability:** shifts, overtime, relocation/travel.
- Activity 11: role-play simulation (interviewer / candidate / observer).

### 2.4 Simulating interviews & assessments
- Practise with friends/family; **group tests**: lead, collaborate, communicate. Group dynamics used: **case resolution**, **role dynamics**, **brainstorming**, **simulation games**, **debates**.
- Show active listening, clear verbal + body language; know sector news; keep LinkedIn active; do networking; consider coaching services.

## 3. Employment in Europe
- EU = 27 countries; free mobility, qualification recognition, programmes like **Erasmus+**. Schengen area: no internal border controls (also Norway, Iceland, Switzerland).
- **Sectors in demand:** tech (AI, cybersecurity, development), healthcare (ageing), renewable energy, education/training (soft skills), tourism (Spain, Italy, Greece…).
- **Valued skills:** technical + languages (**English**), teamwork, adaptability, digital competences.

### 3.1 Recommendations
- Research the country (visa, **recognition of your title**), tailor CV and cover letter per offer, learn the local language / improve English (Duolingo, Babbel; TOEFL/IELTS), use **Europass**, join sector groups.
- Case (María, renewable-energies technician): research countries/companies, register on **EURES**, adapt profile to employers' needs, Europass CV (multilingual), **Erasmus+** internships, online language courses + certificates.

### 3.2 Programmes
- **Europass** (online set of tools for CV, skills, career in Europe), **EURES** (European network: offers, living/working conditions, personal advisers, job fairs), **Erasmus+** (study and *work placements* abroad).
- EU workers/students have right to free movement without special permits; working conditions differ by country (hours, culture).

# Theme 3 – Soft skills, communication, time, teamwork, conflicts

## 1. Soft skills
- Technical skills alone don't guarantee a good worker. McKinsey: demand for social/emotional skills will grow **+24 % by 2030**.
- **Soft skills** = interpersonal, social, emotional competences; transversal (vs. *hard skills* tied to a profession). Partly innate, developed throughout life.
- Most valued: adaptability/resilience, responsibility/commitment, positive & proactive attitude, **emotional intelligence**, teamwork, communication, initiative/proactivity, continuous learning, creativity. Importance varies by role.

## 2. Improving employability competences (DAFO / CAME)
1. **Identify** the competences demanded in your sector (job offers, companies, web).
2. **Self-analysis** → organise in **DAFO/SWOT** (internal: weaknesses & strengths; external: threats & opportunities).
3. **CAME** matrix to act: **C**orregir debilidades · **A**frontar amenazas · **M**antener fortalezas · **E**xplotar oportunidades.
- Keep training, attend events, maintain networking.

## 3. Communication
- Exchange of information, knowledge and emotions → collaboration, results, inclusive culture.

### 3.1 Types
- **Verbal** (oral/written). Steps for a message: analyse the situation (aim, audience, circumstances) → structure it logically and adapt to audience → deliver (rhythm, volume, pronunciation, gestures) → get **feedback**.
  - Written: **clarity, adequate length, veracity, grammar/organisation, good presentation**.
- **Non-verbal:** gestures, posture, images. Studied in 3 disciplines: **kinesics** (body movement, facial expression), **proxemics** (use of space), **paralinguistics** (tone, volume, pauses, speed).
  - 3 rules: read gestures in **clusters** (≥3), look for **congruence** (trust non-verbal when it contradicts words), interpret by **context**.

### 3.2 Barriers
- Obstacles that distort/block understanding. Types: **personal/psychological**, **physical** (noise), **semantic** (jargon, ambiguity, culture), **distortion** (personal filters), **perception barriers**: *halo effect*, *selective perception*, *stereotyping*.
- Case (Ismael & manager): vague message, confusing body language, no feedback invited; Ismael distracted by phone, interrupted, didn't paraphrase.

### 3.3 Active listening (*escucha activa*)
- *Hearing* (oír) is passive; **listening** (escuchar) is intentional. Full attention to words **and** non-verbal cues, to *understand*, not to reply.
- Rules: stay concentrated, don't interrupt, show interest (gestures), give feedback/paraphrase, ask clarifying questions.

## 4. Group dynamics for communication
- **Brainstorming** (Alex Osborn): generate many ideas without criticism; defer judgement, welcome wild ideas, build on others' ideas, quantity first; then select by consensus. Tools: Miro, Mural, Padlet.
- **Philips 6-6:** groups of 6 discuss for 6 min on one topic; includes everyone but time is tight.
- **Role-playing:** act out a situation to understand it, develop empathy; used in selection to observe personality.
- **Outdoor training:** outdoor experiential activities (learning pyramid: we remember far more from doing) → empathy, teamwork, communication, motivation.
  - Case (Pedro): goals = cohesion, communication; activities: *Guide and the blind* (verbal communication/trust), *Water transport* (cooperation), *Rappel & zip-line* (trust/empathy), *Crisis on the mountain* (role-play, decisions, conflict resolution); then feedback session + monthly-reviewed action plan.

## 4(b). Time management
- Benefits: productivity, efficiency, work–life balance, less stress.
- **Tips:** plan with breaks; split tasks; set priorities (**Eisenhower matrix**: urgent/important → do, schedule, delegate, eliminate); realistic calendar; use project tools (Trello, Asana, MS Project, Jira, ClickUp, Planner/Teams); eliminate distractions (phone, social media); delegate.
- **Techniques:** **time boxing** (task in a fixed block, ≤3 h) · **time blocking** (group similar tasks into calendar blocks) · **Pomodoro** (short work bursts + breaks) · **GTD – Getting Things Done** (David Allen: write everything down, then order/prioritise) · **"Eat the frog"** (hardest task first) · **Pareto 80/20** (80 % of results from 20 % of causes).

## 5. Teamwork
- All teams are groups, but not all groups are teams. Team vs. group: shared commitment, interdependence, joint tasks and goals, collective + individual responsibility → **synergy**.
- Pros: productivity, learning, skills (negotiation, decisions), commitment, better communication. Cons: can reduce individual effort, slower, conformity pressure, conflicts, rumours/misunderstandings, goals missed with poor coordination.
- **5.1 Effective-team features:** communication, coordination, complementarity, trust, commitment.
- **5.2 Phases:** (1) first contact → (2) goals/norms/tasks → (3) first difficulties & conflicts → (4) consolidation & efficiency → (5) exhaustion / project closure.
- **5.3 Belbin roles** (9, in 3 categories; ideally all present): **Action** (Impulsor/Shaper, Implementador/Implementer, Finalizador/Completer-Finisher) · **Social** (Coordinador, Investigador/Resource Investigator, Cohesionador/Teamworker) · **Mental/thinking** (analyses ideas, e.g. Cerebro/Plant, Monitor-Evaluator, Specialist).
- **5.4 Work meetings:** planned encounter to discuss, plan, decide. Needs clear topic + agenda; **ideal 5–7 attendees**; short (≈30 min informative, up to ~2 h for working ones) with breaks; fixed at low-disruption times. In-person (stronger connection, commitment) vs. online (accessible, flexible, cheaper; "Zoom fatigue"). **Phases:** planning → preparation (agenda, convocation, venue/resources) → development (objectives, rules, moderated discussion, close each point) → follow-up (minutes, actions).
- **5.5 Conflict resolution:** conflict = perceived incompatibility of interests/needs/values. Resolving it → collaboration, productivity, satisfaction, better climate.
  - **Mediation** – neutral third party facilitates, parties decide.
  - **Arbitration** – third party hears both sides and imposes a **binding** decision (serious/urgent cases).
  - **Negotiation** – offers/counter-offers until a compromise; best general technique. Requires: **preparation, clear communication, empathy, flexibility, objectivity, patience, commitment**.
  - Activity: two teams (companies) negotiate a collaboration agreement.
